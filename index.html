Why crystals have facets: the Wulff construction

Gibbs (1878): minimise the surface energy

For a crystal of fixed volume in its vapour or solution, the equilibrium shape minimises the total surface free energy Σj γjOj, where γj is the surface energy of face j and Oj its area. Different crystal planes have different surface energies — close-packed planes break the fewest bonds per area and are cheapest — so the crystal cannot simply be a sphere.

Wulff (1901): h = λγ

Wulff stated (and Laue 1943, Dinghas 1943 and Herring 1951 later proved) the geometric rule: in the equilibrium shape, the distance hj from the crystal centre to face j, measured perpendicular to the face, is proportional to its surface energy:

hj = λ · γj

Only ratios of surface energies are geometrically meaningful; the scale factor λ is set by the crystal size. That is why one γ in this app is always fixed as the reference.

The construction (Herring's recipe)

  1. Plot γ as a function of the surface normal direction n̂: the γ-plot (red curve in tab 1).
  2. At every point of the γ-plot, draw the plane perpendicular to the radius vector, at distance γ(n̂) from the origin.
  3. The inner envelope of all these planes is the equilibrium crystal shape.

Cusps make facets; everything between them is missing

γ(n̂) has sharp cusps (minima) at the low-index orientations, because a vicinal surface there costs extra step energy. At a cusp the perpendicular plane cuts into the envelope and survives as a facet. Orientations between cusps are missing: their planes lie outside the envelope and the corresponding surface appears only at edges and corners. In this app the γ-plot between cusps is drawn as the exact support function of the resulting polyhedron — the marginal case, in which inter-cusp orientations touch only at corners. Real crystals have γ + γ″ > 0 between cusps (step–step interactions), which rounds the corners slightly, but the facets and their relative sizes are as constructed here.

3D shapes from ratios

In tab 2 the polyhedron is computed exactly as the intersection of the half-spaces n·r ≤ γ{n} of all enabled plane families. A family contributes facets only if its planes are close enough to the centre; the app reports the critical ratio numerically. Two useful exact results for a crystal bounded by {111} and {100} alone: the {100} facets disappear when γ{100} > √3 · γ{111} ≈ 1.732 (pure octahedron), and the {111} facets disappear when γ{111} > √3 · γ{100} (pure cube).

Equilibrium growth vs. fast growth

The Wulff shape is the equilibrium shape: it is reached when atoms can rearrange on the surface, i.e. slow growth or long annealing. Under fast growth the shape is instead governed by orientation-dependent growth velocities — the kinetic Wulff construction — and can look completely different (needles, dendrites). In the SupersAPPturated app the growing precipitates were drawn as spheres; here you can see what equilibrium adds: facets.

Simplifications (explicit limits of this teaching model)

  • Sharp facets only — no rounded corners (γ + γ″ > 0 regions are neglected).
  • Zero temperature: no entropy-driven surface roughening, which in reality softens cusps.
  • No adsorbates, solvent, strain or twin boundaries — all of these change γ and hence the shape (this is how shape-control with surfactants or CO works in nanocrystal synthesis).
  • Four-fold symmetry in 2D; cubic symmetry in 3D. Metal γ-ratios are rounded literature values (relativistic DFT and experiments, e.g. Vitos et al. 1998; Tran et al. 2016) and are meant as teaching estimates — edit them.

Wie das Modell funktioniert (für Neugierige)

Die Gleichgewichtsform eines Kristalls minimiert die gesamte Oberflächenenergie Σ γjOj (Gibbs). Der Satz von Wulff sagt: Der Abstand hj einer Facette vom Kristallzentrum ist proportional zu ihrer Oberflächenenergie, hj = λγj. Die Konstruktion: γ(n̂) polar auftragen (das γ-Diagramm), in jeder Richtung die senkrechte Ebene im Abstand γ errichten, und deren innere Hüllfläche bilden — das ist die Gleichgewichtsform. Kerben (Cusps) in γ(n̂) an den Niederindex-Orientierungen werden zu Facetten; die Orientierungen dazwischen fehlen und erscheinen nur an Kanten und Ecken. In Tab 2 wird das Polyeder exakt als Durchschnitt aller Halbräume n·r ≤ γ{n} berechnet; die App zeigt numerisch, bei welchem γ-Verhältnis eine Facettenfamilie verschwindet. Es gelten nur Verhältnisse der γ-Werte (λ ist die Größenskala), deshalb ist jeweils eine Energie als Referenz festgelegt. Die Wulff-Form gilt für Gleichgewicht bzw. langsames Wachstum; schnelles Wachstum folgt der kinetischen Wulff-Konstruktion (Geschwindigkeiten statt Energien) und liefert Nadeln und Dendriten.
